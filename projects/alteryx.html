Business Problem

Finding High-Value Customers for Mobile Pet Grooming

A mobile pet grooming business depends on reaching customers who are willing to pay for convenience, premium pet care, and recurring at-home service. The challenge is that not every pet-owning area is equally valuable. The strongest markets are usually neighborhoods with the right combination of income, lifestyle, pet ownership, service demand, and geographic accessibility.

This project used Alteryx and ArcGIS to evaluate potential trade areas and identify customer segments most likely to match the business model. Instead of marketing broadly across every nearby ZIP code, the goal was to prioritize areas where the business would have the highest chance of attracting repeat, high-value customers.

Analytical Objective

What the Analysis Needed to Answer

Who Are the Best Customers?

Identify customer segments most likely to value premium mobile pet grooming based on income, lifestyle, household profile, and service preferences.

Where Are They Located?

Use geographic analysis to understand where high-potential customers are concentrated and which areas are realistic for mobile service coverage.

Which Trade Areas Matter Most?

Compare markets based on customer concentration, demographic fit, demand potential, and accessibility.

Where Should the Business Focus?

Translate the workflow into a practical targeting recommendation for marketing, routing, and expansion strategy.

Workflow Reasoning

Why Alteryx and ArcGIS Were Used Together

Alteryx was used to clean, combine, filter, and score the data. This made it easier to create a repeatable workflow that could process customer, demographic, and geographic inputs without manually rebuilding the analysis each time.

ArcGIS was used to interpret the spatial side of the problem. Since mobile pet grooming depends on where customers live, the analysis needed more than spreadsheet summaries. Mapping helped evaluate service areas, customer concentration, neighborhood patterns, and trade area boundaries.

Together, the tools supported both sides of the decision: Alteryx handled the data workflow, while ArcGIS helped translate that data into location strategy.

Alteryx Workflow

How the Workflow Was Built

1. Data Input

Imported customer, demographic, and location-based data into Alteryx. The goal was to bring separate data sources into one workflow so they could be evaluated consistently.

2. Data Cleaning

Standardized fields, removed irrelevant records, checked formatting issues, and prepared the data for segmentation and geographic analysis.

3. Customer Segmentation

Grouped customers and market areas based on characteristics connected to mobile pet grooming demand, such as income, lifestyle fit, household profile, and premium service potential.

4. Trade Area Scoring

Compared geographic areas using segment fit, market opportunity, and customer concentration to identify priority trade areas for outreach and service planning.

ArcGIS Analysis

Key Findings

What the Analysis Revealed

High-Income Areas Were Not Enough

Income mattered, but the strongest trade areas also needed lifestyle fit, pet ownership potential, and convenience-driven demand.

Customer Segments Changed the Strategy

Segment analysis helped separate attractive markets from areas that looked strong demographically but were less aligned with mobile grooming behavior.

Geography Affected Profitability

Because the business travels to customers, distance and service coverage mattered. A strong customer area was less useful if it created inefficient routing.

Targeting Beat Broad Marketing

The analysis supported a more focused strategy: prioritize the best-fit trade areas first instead of spending marketing budget evenly across all nearby neighborhoods.

Business Recommendation

Recommended Targeting Strategy

The final recommendation was to focus marketing and service coverage on trade areas with stronger concentrations of high-value customer segments. These areas offered a better combination of income, lifestyle fit, pet-care demand, and realistic mobile service access.

For the mobile pet grooming business, this meant prioritizing neighborhoods where customers were more likely to value convenience and recurring premium service. The workflow also helped avoid wasting resources on broad areas that may have had population density but weaker customer fit.

From a business perspective, the project turned market data into a practical decision framework for customer targeting, route planning, promotional focus, and future expansion.
