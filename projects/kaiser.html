Executive Summary

Business Problem

Kaiser Permanente's Membership Administration Reporting System (MARS) supports dozens of reporting, analytics, and operational service teams responsible for delivering critical data products across the organization. As MARS prepared for its transition to MARS 2.0, leadership needed greater visibility into workload distribution, team capacity, turnaround times, and future staffing requirements.

Existing reporting provided limited insight into how work moved across teams, where operational bottlenecks existed, and whether current staffing levels could support future demand. Multi-team workflows often experienced longer completion times, while utilization levels varied significantly across functional groups, making resource planning increasingly difficult.

Our objective was to develop a data-driven workforce planning framework that could help leadership understand current operational performance, identify capacity constraints, quantify staffing gaps, and evaluate organizational design recommendations to support a more scalable operating model.

Analytical Workflow

Methodology

1. Data Validation

Validated CRM intake records by identifying missing fields, inconsistent statuses, and illogical timestamp sequences. Standardized lifecycle stages to create a reliable analytical dataset.

2. Turnaround Time Analysis

Engineered assignment, pre-work, execution, closure, and total turnaround time metrics to understand where delays occurred throughout the request lifecycle.

3. Capacity & Utilization Modeling

Combined workload demand with estimated team capacity to evaluate utilization levels, identify resource constraints, and quantify staffing gaps across operational groups.

4. Forecasting & Organizational Design

Projected future workload demand and evaluated organizational restructuring scenarios to support a more scalable workforce planning strategy under MARS 2.0.

Recommendation Logic

How the Analysis Informed the Core-Flex Model

The analysis showed that the issue was not simply a lack of headcount. Some teams were consistently operating above capacity, while others had more flexible bandwidth. At the same time, requests involving multiple teams created longer delays because ownership, handoffs, and coordination were less predictable.

Based on those patterns, the recommendation focused on a Core-Flex staffing model: core resources for stable, recurring work; flexible delivery support for teams with demand spikes; and floating capacity to absorb cross-team or specialized requests. This made the staffing recommendation more realistic than simply adding headcount everywhere.
