Executive Summary

Business Problem

Kaiser Permanente's Membership Administration Reporting System (MARS) supports reporting, analytics, and operational service teams that deliver critical data products across the organization. As MARS prepared for its transition to MARS 2.0, leadership needed a clearer view of workload demand, team capacity, turnaround time, and staffing requirements.

The challenge was not just understanding how much work was coming in. Leadership also needed to understand where work was getting delayed, which teams were operating above sustainable capacity, and how future demand could affect the operating model.

Our objective was to turn operational intake data into a workforce planning framework that could support capacity planning, staffing recommendations, and organizational design decisions.

Analytical Framework

Methodology

Data Validation

Cleaned CRM intake records by checking missing fields, inconsistent statuses, duplicate records, and timestamp sequences across the request lifecycle.

Turnaround Time Analysis

Broke request completion time into assignment, pre-work, execution, closure, and total turnaround time to identify where delays were occurring.

Capacity & Utilization Modeling

Compared forecasted request demand against estimated team capacity to identify utilization pressure, capacity gaps, and staffing risk.

Forecasting & Workforce Planning

Used historical intake patterns to estimate future workload demand and evaluate how MARS 2.0 could affect team capacity and resource allocation.

Decision Framework

Recommendation Logic

The analysis showed that the problem was not simply "add more people." Some teams were consistently above capacity, while others had more flexibility. At the same time, work that crossed multiple teams created longer turnaround times because ownership, handoffs, and dependencies were harder to manage.

Based on those patterns, the recommendation focused on a Core-Flex staffing model. Core resources would support stable, recurring work. Flexible delivery support would absorb demand spikes, specialized requests, and temporary workload imbalances. This made the recommendation more practical than applying the same staffing solution to every team.
